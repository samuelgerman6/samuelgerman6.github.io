Samuel German — Research

I work in theoretical computer science, especially parameterized complexity, automata theory, and computability-theoretic learning. A common theme in my work is obtaining exact structural characterizations, such as tractability and hardness thresholds for discrete problems and accepting-state spectra for automata operations.

I also care about teaching and mathematical exposition; I served for a year as a tutor on the course staff for UC Berkeley’s CS 70, Discrete Mathematics and Probability Theory.

Selected Publications

The Path-Extremal Conjecture for Zero Forcing: Distance-Hereditary Graphs and a Split-Decomposition Reduction

Samuel German.
Accepted to the International Computing and Combinatorics Conference (COCOON 2026); proceedings version to appear in Springer LNCS.

arXiv

Proves the zero-forcing path-extremal conjecture for distance-hereditary graphs and develops a split-decomposition reduction that reduces the unique-prime-bag case to finite verification of bounded split-prime cores.

Strong Conflict-Free Vertex-Connection via Twin Cover: Kernelization and Chromatic Bounds

Samuel German.
Accepted to the International Computing and Combinatorics Conference (COCOON 2026); proceedings version to appear in Springer LNCS.

arXiv

Gives a kernel for deciding whether the strong conflict-free vertex-connection number of \(G\) is at most \(k\), parameterized by the twin-cover number of \(G\) and the target number of colors \(k\). It also proves that for a twin cover \(X\), the strong conflict-free vertex-connection number lies between the chromatic number \(\chi(G)\) and \(\chi(G)+|X|\), and derives an exact formula for \(\chi(G)\) in terms of colorings of \(G[X]\).

Layer-Based Width for PAFP

Samuel German.
Accepted to the International Workshop on Combinatorial Algorithms (IWOCA 2026); invited to submit to a special issue of the journal Discrete Mathematics & Theoretical Computer Science (DMTCS). Proceedings version to appear in Springer LNCS.

arXiv

Introduces layer-based width parameters for the Path Avoiding Forbidden Pairs problem via both the union digraph \(G \cup \mathcal{F}\) and the input graph \(G\), and proves a sharp union-digraph contrast: bounded union BFS-width together with bounded backward arcs yields an FPT algorithm, whereas bounded union BFS-width alone remains \(\mathsf{NP}\text{-}\)hard already at width \(2\). On the input-graph side, it gives the first tractability regimes for PAFP with unrestricted forbidden pairs, including FPT for BFS-width \(2\) with bounded backward input arcs and polynomial time for exact-length width \(2\), both tight against width-\(3\) hardness.
